# Your first 90 days as a BA

A practical starter plan for your first three months in an ICT company. Adjust it to your situation. If you're dropped straight into a live project, compress days 1–30 into your first two weeks.

## Days 1–30: Learn

**Goal:** understand the company, how it delivers projects, and the client or domain.

| Week | Do this |
|------|---------|
| 1 | Meet your manager and agree expectations. Get access to tools (Jira, Confluence, Teams/Slack, diagram tools). Read the company's delivery methodology and templates. |
| 2 | Meet the people you'll work with: PMs, other BAs, architects, QA leads. Ask each one: *"What do you need from a BA? What do BAs do that frustrates you?"* |
| 3 | Read existing project documents: a business case, a BRD, a backlog, a closure report. Notice what good looks like. |
| 4 | Learn the client's business: their products, customers, processes, jargon. Start your own glossary. Shadow a senior BA in a workshop. |

**By day 30 you should be able to:** explain how your company runs a project, name the key people, and describe your client's business in two minutes.

## Days 31–60: Contribute

**Goal:** produce real, reviewed work.

| Do this | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Take ownership of a small piece: an epic, a feature, or a set of stories | Learn by doing, with a safety net |
| Run your first interview or small workshop (with a senior BA observing) | Build facilitation confidence |
| Draw an as-is and to-be process map | The most reusable BA skill |
| Write acceptance criteria and walk them through with a developer and tester (Three Amigos) | Learn what "testable" really means |
| Ask for feedback on every document you produce | Improve fast |

**By day 60 you should be able to:** run a session independently, write stories the team can build from, and keep a requirements log up to date.

## Days 61–90: Own

**Goal:** be trusted as the BA for a workstream.

| Do this | Why |
|---------|-----|
| Own requirements for a feature end to end: elicitation → sign-off → build support → UAT | Experience the full cycle |
| Handle at least one change request with impact analysis | Core BA skill under pressure |
| Support UAT for something you specified | See your requirements meet reality |
| Run a mini-retrospective on your own work | Build the habit of reflecting |
| Agree a development plan with your manager (skills, certification) | Plan your growth |

**By day 90 you should be:** the go-to person for your feature's requirements.

## Habits to build from day one

- **Write it down and send it back.** Confirm every meeting's outcomes within 24 hours.
- **Ask "why?"** more than feels comfortable, politely.
- **Draw before you write.** A diagram often replaces two pages.
- **Keep a decisions log.** Who decided what, when, and why.
- **Never guess a business rule.** Ask, then document the answer.
- **Separate facts, assumptions and opinions** in your notes.
- **Be visible.** Share progress and blockers early.

## Questions you can always ask in a new project

1. What problem are we solving, and for whom?
2. How will we know we succeeded?
3. Who decides priorities and signs off requirements?
4. What's in scope, and what's explicitly out?
5. What constraints do we have (time, budget, technology, regulation)?
6. Who else should I talk to?
