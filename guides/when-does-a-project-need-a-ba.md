# When does a project need a BA, and why it matters

[← RACI](../roles/raci-who-does-what.md) · [🏠 Contents](../README.md) · [Next: The BA in Agile vs Waterfall →](ba-in-agile-vs-waterfall.md)

---

Not every piece of ICT work needs a dedicated Business Analyst. A server patch or a simple bug fix doesn't. But some projects **must** have one, and some situations call for the BA to **lead** the work rather than just support it.

## Signals that a project needs a BA

Score the project. The more "yes" answers, the more a BA is essential.

| # | Question | Yes? |
|---|----------|------|
| 1 | Is the problem unclear, or is the client asking for a solution without explaining the need? | |
| 2 | Are there **multiple stakeholder groups** with different (possibly conflicting) needs? | |
| 3 | Will **business processes change** for the people using the system? | |
| 4 | Does it **integrate** with other systems or replace a legacy system? | |
| 5 | Are there **regulatory, legal, privacy or security** requirements? | |
| 6 | Is it a **fixed-price** or contract-based engagement where scope must be precise? | |
| 7 | Is the budget significant enough that building the wrong thing would hurt? | |
| 8 | Does it involve **data migration** or complex business rules? | |
| 9 | Will success be measured by **business outcomes** (not just "it's live")? | |
| 10 | Is the delivery team unfamiliar with the client's business domain? | |

| Score | Recommendation |
|-------|----------------|
| 0–2 | A PM or tech lead can handle light analysis |
| 3–5 | A part-time or shared BA is needed |
| 6–10 | A dedicated BA, involved from the **idea phase** |

## When the BA should *lead*, not just support

There are moments where the BA is the natural person to drive the work:

| Situation | Why the BA leads |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Pre-project discovery / idea phase** | No PM yet; the question is "what's the problem?", which is pure analysis |
| **Feasibility and options analysis** | Requires neutral, evidence-based comparison |
| **Requirements workshops and sign-off** | The BA is the facilitator and owner of the requirements |
| **Process redesign** | The BA understands current and future processes end to end |
| **Change impact analysis** | The BA knows the requirements and their dependencies best |
| **UAT preparation** | Scenarios come from business processes and requirements |
| **Business readiness** | The BA knows what changes for whom |
| **Benefits review** | The BA defined the KPIs and can analyse shortfalls |

## Why the BA role matters: the cost of skipping it

Projects without proper business analysis tend to fail in predictable ways:

| Symptom | Root cause | What a BA would have done |
|---------|-----------|---------------------------|
| "That's not what we asked for" at UAT | Requirements were assumed, not confirmed | Elicitation with play-back and sign-off |
| Endless scope creep | No baseline, no change control | Baseline requirements + change process |
| Features nobody uses | Built what was asked, not what was needed | Root-cause analysis; user validation |
| Budget overrun | Late discovery of complexity (integrations, rules) | Early interface and rules analysis |
| Disputes with the client | Ambiguous scope in the contract | Explicit in/out scope; testable requirements |
| Go-live chaos | Users not trained; processes not updated | Transition requirements; business readiness |
| "Was it worth it?" Nobody knows | No measurable objectives | KPIs in the business case; benefits review |

## The value of a BA to an ICT services company

For your employer specifically, a good BA:

1. **Wins work:** credible discovery and pre-sales analysis builds client trust.
2. **Protects margin:** clear scope makes estimates accurate and change requests billable.
3. **Reduces rework:** defects found in requirements are the cheapest to fix.
4. **Grows accounts:** satisfied clients return, and the BA's deferred backlog often becomes the next project.
5. **Builds domain knowledge:** the BA becomes the go-to person for the client's business.

## How to explain your value in one sentence

When someone asks *"what does a BA actually add?"*, try:

> "I make sure we build the right thing the first time, so the money spent delivers the value the business was promised."
