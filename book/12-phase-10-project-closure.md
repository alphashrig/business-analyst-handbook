## What's happening

The solution is live and stable, and hypercare has ended. The project is **formally closed**:

- Deliverables are accepted and contractual obligations confirmed.
- The team is released.
- Documentation is archived and handed over.
- Lessons learned are captured.
- In an ICT services company: final invoicing, and often the start of a support contract or the next phase.

The **Project Manager owns closure**. The BA provides the evidence that **what was promised was delivered**.

## Why the BA matters here

Closure is where the project proves it did what it set out to do, and where unfinished business is either handed over properly or lost forever. The BA:

- Shows, requirement by requirement, what was delivered, changed, deferred or dropped.
- Makes sure deferred items and enhancement ideas reach a real backlog with an owner.
- Hands over requirements knowledge so future teams don't start from zero.
- Captures analysis-specific lessons that make the next project better.

## Your responsibilities

- [ ] Complete the **final RTM**: every requirement's status (delivered, changed, deferred, withdrawn).
- [ ] Reconcile **scope**: baseline vs delivered, with every approved change listed.
- [ ] Hand over **open items**: deferred requirements, enhancement requests, known issues, with owners.
- [ ] Finalise and **archive documentation**: BRD, process maps, business rules, data dictionary, decisions log.
- [ ] Contribute the **BA section of the closure report**.
- [ ] Facilitate or contribute to the **lessons-learned (retrospective)** session.
- [ ] Confirm the **benefits measurement plan**: who measures which KPI, when, and how.
- [ ] Hand over to the **product / operations owner** for ongoing business analysis.

## Who you work with

| Person | Relationship |
|--------|-------------|
| **PM** | Owns closure report, contract closure, team release; you provide requirements and scope evidence |
| **Sponsor** | Accepts the closure; owns benefits after closure |
| **Product / service owner** | Receives deferred backlog and documentation |
| **Support / operations** | Receives final knowledge base and known issues |
| **Project team** | Lessons-learned session |

## Techniques

| Technique | Use it to... |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Requirements coverage analysis** | Prove delivery against baseline using the RTM |
| **Retrospective / lessons learned** | What went well, what didn't, what to change |
| **Knowledge transfer sessions** | Pass on context that documents can't capture |
| **Benefits realisation plan** | Make sure value gets measured after the team leaves |

### Lessons-learned format (simple and effective)

| Keep doing | Stop doing | Start doing |
|------------|------------|-------------|
| Contact-centre observation day | Requirements sign-off by email chain | DPO in the first workshop |
| Three Amigos before each story | Demo feedback without logging | Prototype tests earlier |

Write lessons as **actions for the next project**, not complaints.

## QuickClaim in this phase

Your final RTM shows:

| Status | Count |
|--------|-------|
| Delivered as baselined | 61 |
| Delivered with approved change | 7 |
| Deferred to phase 2 (approved) | 4 |
| Withdrawn (approved) | 2 |
| **Undelivered without approval** | **0** |

That last line is what the sponsor and your company's account director care about most. There are no disputes about scope.

Phase 2 candidates you hand over to BrightCover's digital product owner: garage booking, claim editing after submission, real-time status (needs legacy API), home insurance claims. Each has its original CR, the impact analysis and the reason it was deferred.

Lessons learned (BA view):
- **Keep:** observation day; UAT scenarios from real claims; Three Amigos.
- **Stop:** discovering legacy-system constraints through interface analysis in week 2. Ask for architecture docs in the idea phase.
- **Start:** involving the DPO and the support team from initiation.

The PM closes the project. Your company and BrightCover start discussing phase 2, and the deferred backlog becomes the first draft of its scope.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
|---------|---------|
| Moving to the next project before closure | Block time for closure; it's part of the job |
| Lessons learned as a blame session | Focus on process and actions, not people |
| Deferred items lost in a spreadsheet | Hand them over to a named owner in a live backlog |
| Documentation left in personal folders | Archive in the agreed shared repository |

## Exit checklist

- [ ] Final RTM complete; every requirement accounted for
- [ ] Deferred items and known issues handed to named owners
- [ ] Documentation archived and accessible
- [ ] Lessons learned captured and shared
- [ ] Benefits measurement owner and dates agreed
- [ ] Sponsor accepts closure
