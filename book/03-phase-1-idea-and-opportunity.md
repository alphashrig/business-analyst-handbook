# Chapter 3: Phase 1, Idea and opportunity

## What's happening

There is no project yet, only an **idea**. It might come from:

- A client conversation ("Our customers keep complaining about the claims process").
- A sales opportunity or an RFP (Request for Proposal).
- An internal pain point, a regulation change, a competitor's move, or new technology.

Ideas at this stage are vague, emotional, and usually phrased as a **solution** ("we need an app", "let's move to the cloud", "we need AI").

## Why the BA matters here

This is the cheapest moment in the whole lifecycle to stop a bad idea or reshape a good one. Without a BA:

- The organisation builds the solution someone asked for instead of the one they need.
- Nobody defines how success will be measured, so nobody can later prove value.
- Sales commits to a scope nobody has analysed, and delivery inherits the problem.

**The BA turns an idea into a clear problem statement and an opportunity worth (or not worth) pursuing.**

## Your responsibilities

- [ ] Find the **problem behind the requested solution** (ask "why?" repeatedly).
- [ ] Identify **who is affected** and how badly.
- [ ] Gather **initial evidence**: data, complaints, metrics, costs.
- [ ] Understand the **current state** at a high level (how it works today).
- [ ] Draft a **problem statement** and **desired outcomes**.
- [ ] Identify obvious **constraints** (budget, deadlines, regulation, technology).
- [ ] Support pre-sales: answer RFP questions, give rough complexity input for estimates.
- [ ] Recommend whether the idea should move to **initiation**.

## Who you work with

| Person | What you need from them | What they need from you |
|--------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Client sponsor or idea owner | Their goals, pain, budget expectations | Clear framing of the problem |
| Account manager / sales | Commercial context, client relationships | Realistic view of scope and complexity |
| Pre-sales solution architect | Technical feasibility view | Business context |
| Subject matter experts (SMEs) | How things really work today | Good questions |

## Techniques

| Technique | Use it to... |
|-----------|--------------|
| **5 Whys** | Get from a requested solution to the root problem |
| **Problem statement** | Write the problem in one clear paragraph (format below) |
| **Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram** | Explore causes of a problem |
| **SWOT / PESTLE** | Understand the organisation and its environment |
| **Benchmarking** | See what competitors and the industry do |
| **Lean canvas / opportunity canvas** | Summarise the idea on one page |

### Problem statement format

> **The problem of** *[describe the problem]*
> **affects** *[who]*,
> **the impact of which is** *[the cost or pain]*.
> **A successful solution would** *[outcomes, not features]*.

## Deliverables

| Deliverable | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| Problem statement | One paragraph, agreed with the idea owner |
| Opportunity summary | 1–2 pages: problem, evidence, outcomes, constraints, recommendation |
| Initial stakeholder list | Who is affected, who decides (feeds the [stakeholder register](../templates/stakeholder-register.md)) |
| Rough current-state sketch | A simple high-level process diagram |

## QuickClaim in this phase

BrightCover's Head of Claims says in a sales meeting: *"We need a mobile app for claims."*

You ask **why** (5 Whys):

1. *Why an app?* "Customers hate calling us."
2. *Why do they hate calling?* "Average wait is 38 minutes after an accident."
3. *Why so long?* "Every claim needs a 25-minute call to collect details."
4. *Why 25 minutes?* "Agents type everything manually: photos are emailed separately, policy is looked up in another system."
5. *Why does that matter?* "Customer satisfaction for claims is 54%, we're losing renewals, and the contact centre costs keep rising."

Your problem statement:

> **The problem of** slow, phone-only first notification of motor claims
> **affects** policyholders who have just had an accident, and contact-centre agents,
> **the impact of which is** 38-minute wait times, a 54% claims satisfaction score, lost renewals and rising contact-centre cost.
> **A successful solution would** let customers report a claim in under 10 minutes, at any time, with photos and policy details captured automatically, and reduce claims calls by at least 40%.

Notice that the statement doesn't say "mobile app". A mobile app may be the answer, but so might a web form or a WhatsApp flow. You keep options open for the next phase.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Instead |
|---------|---------|
| Accepting "we need an app" as the requirement | Ask why until you reach the business problem |
| Jumping into detailed requirements | Stay high-level; the project isn't approved yet |
| Talking only to the sponsor | Get at least one view from people who do the work |
| No numbers | Find at least one metric that shows the pain |
| Promising scope to help close the sale | Give ranges and assumptions, never commitments |

## Exit checklist

- [ ] Problem statement agreed with the idea owner
- [ ] At least one measurable outcome identified
- [ ] Key stakeholders and constraints listed
- [ ] Clear recommendation: proceed to initiation, rethink, or stop
