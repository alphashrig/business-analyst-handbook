# Business Analyst vs Project Manager

[🏠 Contents](../README.md) · [Next: BA vs Product Owner →](ba-vs-product-owner.md)

---

This is the most confused pair of roles in ICT. On small projects one person may do both, which makes it even more important to know which hat you're wearing.

## The short version

> The **Project Manager** makes sure the project delivers **on time, on budget, and within scope**.
> The **Business Analyst** makes sure the project delivers **the right thing**: the solution that solves the business problem.

Or, more simply:

| | Project Manager | Business Analyst |
|-|-----------------|------------------|
| Focus | **How** and **when** the project gets done | **What** gets done and **why** |
| Measures success by | Delivered on time, budget, scope, quality | Solution meets the business need; benefits realised |
| Primary concern | The **project** | The **product / solution** and the **business** |

## Side-by-side comparison

| Area | Project Manager | Business Analyst |
|------|-----------------|------------------|
| **Owns** | Project plan, schedule, budget, resources, risks, issues, project communications | Requirements, business analysis artefacts, traceability |
| **Key question** | "Are we on track?" | "Are we building the right thing?" |
| **Scope** | Manages the scope *baseline* and controls changes to it | Defines and details *what* is in scope; analyses change impact on the business |
| **Stakeholders** | Manages expectations about delivery, status, escalations | Engages stakeholders to understand needs and reach agreement on requirements |
| **Risk** | Owns the risk register and mitigations | Identifies business and requirements risks; feeds the PM's log |
| **Change requests** | Assesses schedule and cost impact; runs the change process | Assesses business and requirements impact; documents the change |
| **Team** | Plans and allocates work; removes blockers | Guides the team on business needs; answers questions |
| **Quality** | Ensures quality processes are followed | Ensures requirements are testable; supports UAT |
| **Typical artefacts** | Project charter, plan, Gantt / roadmap, RAID log, status reports, closure report | Business case analysis, BRD, user stories, process models, RTM, UAT scenarios |
| **Typical skills** | Planning, scheduling, budgeting, risk, negotiation, leadership | Elicitation, analysis, modelling, facilitation, writing, critical thinking |
| **Certifications** | PMP, PRINCE2, CAPM, PMI-ACP | CBAP, CCBA, ECBA (IIBA), PMI-PBA, BCS Business Analysis |
| **Reports to (often)** | PMO / delivery head | BA practice lead / delivery head (on project: works with the PM) |

## Who does what, phase by phase

| Phase | Project Manager | Business Analyst |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|
| Idea | Usually not yet assigned | Frames the problem, gathers evidence |
| Initiation | Writes the charter; estimates cost and time | Options analysis, feasibility, benefits, high-level scope |
| Planning | Builds the project plan | Builds the BA plan; feeds BA tasks into the project plan |
| Requirements | Protects schedule; manages stakeholder availability | Elicits, analyses, documents, gets sign-off |
| Design | Tracks progress; manages design risks | Reviews design against requirements |
| Build | Tracks sprints / milestones; manages blockers and changes | Refines backlog; answers questions; impact analysis |
| Test | Manages test schedule and go-live date | UAT planning and support; defect triage |
| Deploy | Runs go/no-go; coordinates release | Business readiness, training, hypercare |
| Close | Closure report, contract, team release | Final RTM, scope reconciliation, BA lessons learned |
| Benefits | Usually gone | Measures and analyses benefits (if assigned) |

## Where they overlap (and how to handle it)

| Overlap area | Healthy split |
|--------------|---------------|
| **Stakeholder management** | PM manages expectations about **delivery**; BA engages about **needs**. Share one stakeholder register. |
| **Scope** | BA defines **what scope means** in detail; PM **controls** the scope baseline and change process. |
| **Change requests** | BA writes the CR and business impact; PM adds time/cost impact; sponsor decides. |
| **Risk** | BA spots requirement risks; PM owns the register and mitigations. |
| **Status reporting** | PM reports overall status; BA reports requirements status (e.g. % signed off, open questions). |

## Typical conflicts and how to resolve them

| Situation | PM says | BA says | Resolution |
|-----------|---------|---------|------------|
| Requirements are taking too long | "We need sign-off this week or we'll miss the date." | "Three key questions are unanswered; signing now means rework later." | Sign off what's ready, list open items with owners and dates, and agree which stories can start. Escalate if needed. |
| Client asks for a "small" change in a demo | "No changes, we're on a deadline." | "It might be small and valuable." | Neither decides. Log a CR, analyse impact together, let the sponsor/PO decide. |
| Time is short | "Cut scope." | "Cutting that breaks the business process." | BA shows which requirements are Must vs Should and what each cut breaks; PO decides. |

The PM and BA are **partners**, not competitors. The best projects have a PM and BA who talk every day and back each other up in front of the client.

## When one person does both

On small projects, a "BA/PM" hybrid is common. Tips:

- **Time-box** analysis and management work separately in your week.
- **Be explicit** about which hat you're wearing in meetings.
- Watch out for the main risk: under delivery pressure, the PM hat tends to cut analysis short. Protect elicitation time.
- Get a **peer review** of requirements from another BA if possible.

## QuickClaim example

| Moment | PM did | BA did |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Legacy batch constraint found | Logged the risk, adjusted the plan, informed the steering committee | Found it in interface analysis, explained the business impact, prepared options |
| Garage booking request | Assessed +3 sprints and cost; ran the CR process | Wrote the CR and business impact analysis; recorded the deferral |
| Go-live | Ran go/no-go; coordinated the release | Presented business readiness; ran hypercare triage |
