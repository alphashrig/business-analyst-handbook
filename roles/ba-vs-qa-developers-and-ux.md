# Business Analyst vs QA, Developers and UX

[← BA vs Systems Analyst and Solution Architect](ba-vs-systems-analyst-and-solution-architect.md) · [🏠 Contents](../README.md) · [Next: RACI, who does what →](raci-who-does-what.md)

---

The delivery team is where your requirements become a real product. Knowing each role's job, and what they need from you, makes you the BA every team wants.

## BA vs QA / Tester

| | Business Analyst | QA / Tester |
|-|------------------|-------------|
| **Question** | Is this the **right** thing to build? | Was the thing built **right**? |
| **Validates** | Requirements against business needs | The system against requirements |
| **Owns** | Requirements, acceptance criteria (with PO) | Test strategy, test cases, system/regression testing, defect reports |
| **In UAT** | Plans and supports UAT with the business | Supports the environment, data and defect process |
| **Shared ground** | Acceptance criteria, traceability, defect triage | Same |

**What QA needs from you:** testable acceptance criteria, business rules with examples, edge cases, quick answers on "expected behaviour".
**What you need from QA:** early review of your requirements for testability, coverage reports, clear defect descriptions.

> Tip: invite QA to review requirements *before* sign-off. Testers are brilliant at finding ambiguity.

## BA vs Developer / Tech Lead

| | Business Analyst | Developer / Tech Lead |
|-|------------------|----------------------|
| **Question** | What does the business need? | How do we build and maintain it well? |
| **Owns** | Requirements | Code, technical design detail, estimates, technical quality |
| **Decides** | Nothing technical | Implementation approach (within the architecture) |
| **Shared ground** | Story refinement, clarifications, feasibility | Same |

**What developers need from you:** context (the "why"), clear acceptance criteria, fast answers, stable requirements during a sprint, no surprise changes.
**What you need from developers:** early warnings about complexity, honest estimates, questions *before* guessing.

> Tip: **Three Amigos.** Before a story enters a sprint, a BA, a developer and a tester spend 15 minutes discussing it. Most misunderstandings die here.

## BA vs UX / UI Designer

| | Business Analyst | UX / UI Designer |
|-|------------------|------------------|
| **Focus** | Business needs, rules, processes, data | User experience, interaction, visual design, usability |
| **Research** | Stakeholder elicitation, process analysis | User research, usability testing, personas, journey maps |
| **Output** | Requirements, process models, rules | Wireframes, prototypes, UI designs, design systems |
| **Shared ground** | Personas, journeys, prototypes, validation with users | Same |

The BA and UX designer should be **close partners**. The BA brings the rules and constraints ("photos must be tagged by damage area"); the designer finds the best way for a stressed person at a roadside to do it.

**Avoid:** BAs designing screens in detail ("a green button top-right"). Describe the need; let UX design the interaction.

## BA vs Scrum Master

| | Business Analyst | Scrum Master |
|-|------------------|--------------|
| **Focus** | The content of the work (requirements) | The process of the work (how the team works) |
| **Helps the team by** | Clarifying what to build | Removing impediments, coaching Scrum, facilitating events |

## BA vs Change Manager / Trainer

On bigger projects, a **change manager** handles the people side of change (communications, training, adoption). The BA provides the "what changes for whom" analysis (stakeholder impact assessment, process changes). On smaller projects, the BA often does both.

## BA vs Data Analyst

| | Business Analyst | Data Analyst |
|-|------------------|--------------|
| **Focus** | Business problems and solution requirements | Analysing data to produce insights and reports |
| **Uses data to** | Support decisions and requirements | Answer business questions directly |

They overlap in benefits measurement and reporting requirements. Many BAs build data skills to move between both.
