# Business Analyst vs Product Owner

[← BA vs Project Manager](ba-vs-project-manager.md) · [🏠 Contents](../README.md) · [Next: BA vs Systems Analyst and Solution Architect →](ba-vs-systems-analyst-and-solution-architect.md)

---

In Agile (especially Scrum) teams, the **Product Owner (PO)** owns the product backlog. Many new BAs then ask: *"If the PO owns the backlog, what do I do?"*

## The short version

> The **Product Owner** decides **what is most valuable** and **in what order** to build it. They are accountable for maximising the product's value.
> The **Business Analyst** does the **analysis** that makes those decisions well-informed and turns them into clear, buildable, testable requirements.

The PO is a **decision-maker**. The BA is an **analyst and facilitator**. On a healthy team they work as a pair.

## Side-by-side comparison

| Area | Product Owner | Business Analyst |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| **Accountable for** | Product value; backlog ordering; accepting work | Quality of requirements and analysis |
| **Decides** | Priorities, scope trade-offs, what's "done" from a business view | Nothing on priorities alone; recommends based on analysis |
| **Backlog** | Owns it | Writes, refines and maintains items in it |
| **Stakeholders** | Represents them as one voice; resolves conflicts | Elicits from them; surfaces and analyses conflicts |
| **Vision** | Sets the product vision and roadmap | Helps shape it with analysis (market, process, data) |
| **Detail level** | Mostly the "what" and "why" | Down to rules, data, edge cases, acceptance criteria |
| **Time on team** | Ideally available daily, but often part-time from the business | Usually full-time with the team |
| **Usually comes from** | The client / business side | The ICT company or an internal BA practice |
| **Certifications** | CSPO, PSPO | CBAP, CCBA, PMI-PBA, IIBA-AAC (Agile Analysis) |

## How they work together in a sprint cycle

| Activity | PO | BA |
|----------|----|----|
| Product vision and roadmap | Owns | Contributes analysis |
| Creating epics | Approves | Drafts from analysis |
| Splitting into stories | Reviews | Leads |
| Writing acceptance criteria | Agrees | Writes |
| Backlog ordering | **Decides** | Advises on dependencies and value |
| Sprint planning | Presents priorities | Explains stories; answers questions |
| During the sprint | Makes decisions on questions | Answers clarifications; escalates decisions |
| Sprint review | Accepts or rejects work | Captures feedback; turns it into backlog items |

## Common arrangements in ICT companies

| Setup | Where you see it | BA's role |
|-------|------------------|-----------|
| **Client PO + vendor BA** | Most ICT services projects | BA does analysis; client PO decides. The most common setup. |
| **BA acts as proxy PO** | Client PO has little time | BA prepares decisions; client PO approves in short regular sessions. Risky: get decisions in writing. |
| **BA becomes the PO** | Product companies, internal IT | The person carries both roles; must balance deciding and analysing |
| **No BA, only PO** | Small teams | PO does the analysis too, which often means less depth on rules and edge cases |

## Watch out for

- **"BA as backlog secretary":** just typing what the PO says. Push for real analysis: process, data, rules, edge cases.
- **"BA as hidden PO":** making priority decisions because the PO is absent. Make the gap visible to the PM or Scrum Master instead.
- **Bypassing the PO:** stakeholders coming to you with priority requests. Analyse them, then route them to the PO.

## QuickClaim example

The Claims Operations Manager was named PO. When Marketing and the DPO disagreed about collecting marketing data in the claim flow, **you** analysed both positions and the regulation; **the PO** decided "Won't have". When the agent workspace stories slipped down the backlog, **you** flagged the launch dependency; **the PO** reordered the backlog.
